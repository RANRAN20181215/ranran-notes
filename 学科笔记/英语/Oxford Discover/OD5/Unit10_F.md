# BQ5: What do different cultures give to the world? — Unit 10 F

## Big Question
What do different cultures give to the world?

## Reading Genre
Fiction — a story about cultural exchange or a journey between cultures

## Vocabulary Review
Apply Unit 9 vocab in narrative: culture, tradition, custom, cuisine, contribution, ancient, invention

## Grammar in Context: Relative Clauses

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Defining (no commas) | *The dish **that** she cooked was from her grandmother's recipe.* |
| Non-defining (with commas) | *Her grandmother, **who** was from Mexico, taught her to cook.* |
| Omitting the pronoun | *The tradition [**that**] they celebrated was called Día de Muertos.* |

### Prepositions in relative clauses
- *The festival **about which** she wrote was fascinating.* (formal)
- *The festival **which** she wrote **about** was fascinating.* (common)

## Word Study: Borrowed Words
English has borrowed words from many cultures:
- **Arabic**: algebra, algorithm, zero, coffee
- **French**: ballet, café, restaurant
- **Italian**: piano, opera, spaghetti
- **Japanese**: sushi, karate, kimono
- **Hindi**: yoga, jungle, shampoo

## Writing: A Cultural Comparison Essay
1. Choose two cultures
2. Compare: food, celebrations, inventions, traditions
3. Use relative clauses to add detail

### Compare/contrast language
*both, similarly, in contrast, whereas, unlike, on the other hand*

## Example Sentences
> The festival, which takes place every spring, attracts visitors from all over.
> Marco Polo was the explorer who brought pasta from China to Italy.
> Yoga, which originated in India, is now practiced worldwide.
