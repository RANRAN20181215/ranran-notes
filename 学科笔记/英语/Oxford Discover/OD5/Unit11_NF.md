# BQ6: Why are mountains important? — Unit 11 NF

## Big Question
Why are mountains important?

## Vocabulary
- **mountain** — a very high natural hill
- **peak** — the pointed top of a mountain
- **summit** — the highest point of a mountain
- **range** — a line of mountains
- **glacier** — a slow-moving mass of ice
- **erosion** — wearing away of land by wind/water
- **tectonic** — relating to the earth's plates
- **volcano** — a mountain that erupts with lava
- **altitude** — height above sea level
- **climate** — usual weather in a region
- **ecosystem** — a community of living things
- **mineral** — a natural substance in the earth
- **oxygen** — gas that living things breathe

## Reading Strategy
Understanding cause and effect in geography

## Grammar: Conditionals

| Type | Use | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | General truth | If + pres, pres | *If it rains a lot, glaciers grow.* |
| 1 | Real future | If + pres, will + inf | *If we climb higher, the air will be thinner.* |
| 2 | Unreal present | If + past, would + inf | *If I lived near a mountain, I would hike every day.* |
| 3 | Unreal past | If + past perfect, would have + p.p. | *If the volcano had erupted, the village would have been destroyed.* |

### Mixing conditionals
- *If tectonic plates move (0), erosion happens (0).*
- *If the glacier melts (1), sea levels will rise (1).*

## Writing Focus
A cause and effect essay about mountains and their importance

## Example Sentences
> If tectonic plates collide, mountains are formed.
> If the glacier continues to melt, the river will flood.
> If I were a geologist, I would study volcanoes.
> If the mountain had been lower, the snow would have melted.
