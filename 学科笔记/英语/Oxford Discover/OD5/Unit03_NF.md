# BQ2: What are teeth for? — Unit 3 NF

## Big Question
What are teeth for?

## Vocabulary
- **incisor** — front tooth for cutting
- **canine** — pointed tooth for tearing
- **molar** — back tooth for grinding
- **carnivore** — animal that eats meat
- **herbivore** — animal that eats plants
- **omnivore** — animal that eats both
- **enamel** — hard outer layer of a tooth
- **jaw** — bone that holds teeth
- **gums** — flesh around teeth
- **cavity** — hole in a tooth from decay
- **root** — part of tooth below the gum
- **dental** — relating to teeth
- **slice** — cut into thin pieces
- **grip** — hold firmly
- **crush** — press with force to break

## Reading Strategy
Identifying cause and effect — *why* teeth are shaped differently

## Grammar: Modals of Ability

| Modal | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **can** | present ability | *Carnivores can tear meat with their canines.* |
| **could** | past ability | *Early humans could grind seeds with their molars.* |
| **be able to** | ability (any tense) | *Herbivores are able to crush plants.* |

### Form
- can/could + base verb *(can slice, could grip)*
- be able to + base verb *(is able to crush, was able to survive)*

### Note
- **could** = general past ability
- **was/were able to** = specific past success (*He was able to fix his tooth.*)

## Writing Focus
A descriptive paragraph comparing animal teeth and diets

## Example Sentences
> Lions can grip and tear meat with their sharp canines.
> Horses are able to grind grass with their flat molars.
> I could not chew properly when I had a cavity.
